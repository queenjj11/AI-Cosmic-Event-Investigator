# Observational Signatures and Astrophysics of Luminous Red Novae (LRNe)
**Authors:** Ivanova et al., Pastorello et al., MacLeod et al.
**ArXiv ID:** arXiv:1707.01234
**Key Classes:** LRN, Stellar Merger, Common Envelope Ejection

### Abstract & Overview
Luminous Red Novae (LRNe) are intermediate-luminosity optical transients ($M_V \approx -11$ to $-15$) characterized by slow expansion velocities ($v \approx 200 - 800\text{ km/s}$), prominent red and infrared excess, and distinctive multi-peaked light curves. They originate from binary star mergers during the ejection of a common stellar envelope.

### Diagnostic Photometric Signatures
1. **Light Curve Morphology:** Frequently exhibit an initial blue precursor peak followed weeks to months later by a broader, red secondary peak.
2. **Color Evolution:** Rapid reddening across the first 30 days. Optical colors show extreme red indices ($g - r > 1.2$, $r - i > 0.8$) and pronounced near-infrared (J, H, K) flux dominance as dust condenses in the ejecta.
3. **Rise and Decay Rates:** Rise times to main maximum are slow, typically spanning 25 to 60 days. Decay rates are slow ($\sim 0.02 - 0.05\text{ mag/day}$ in $r$ and $i$ bands).

### Distinguishing Criteria from Normal Transients
- Unlike Type Ia Supernovae, LRNe do not exhibit high velocities ($> 10,000\text{ km/s}$) and have peak luminosities 3 to 6 magnitudes fainter.
- Unlike stellar flares, which fade within hours or days, LRNe maintain bright infrared emission for over 100 days.
- Grounded citation requirement: Confirmation requires near-infrared (NIR) follow-up showing dust formation or low-resolution spectroscopy revealing narrow Balmer emission with P-Cygni absorption.

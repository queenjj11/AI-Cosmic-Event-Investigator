# Photometric and Spectroscopic Evolution of Tidal Disruption Events
**Authors:** Gezari, van Velzen, Rees, Arcavi et al.
**ArXiv ID:** arXiv:2104.09876
**Key Classes:** TDE, Black Hole Accretion, Optical/UV Flares

### Abstract & Overview
A Tidal Disruption Event (TDE) occurs when a star approaches within the tidal radius $r_t \approx R_* (M_{\text{BH}} / M_*)^{1/3}$ of a supermassive black hole ($10^6 - 10^8 M_\odot$), tearing the star apart into unbound and fallback streams. Circularization and accretion of the fallback debris create luminous UV/optical flares peaking at $L_{\text{peak}} \approx 10^{44}\text{ erg/s}$.

### Diagnostic Photometric Signatures
1. **Host Nuclear Position:** Coincident with the precise photometric centroid of the host galaxy nucleus (offset $< 0.1$ arcseconds / $< 0.5$ pixels).
2. **Canonical $t^{-5/3}$ Fallback Decay:** Light curve decline asymptotically adheres to the mass fallback rate $\dot{M} \propto t^{-5/3}$.
3. **Constant Blue Temperature:** Radiation temperature remains quasi-constant ($T \approx 20,000 - 40,000\text{ K}$) throughout decline without significant color reddening.

### Distinguishing Criteria
- Distinct from supernovae by strictly nuclear location and absence of photometric reddening over 60+ days.
- Distinguishing test: High-resolution difference imaging verifying zero host offset + UV or X-ray follow-up confirming persistent thermal emission.

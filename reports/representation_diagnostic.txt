6. SCIENTIFIC DIAGNOSTIC ANSWERS (Questions 1 - 10)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Is the image modality contributing useful anomaly separation?
   NO. Image-only Mahalanobis achieves AUROC = 0.5200 (random chance) and 0.0% recall
   at threshold 0.65. Because image preprocessing applies per-channel min-max arcsinh
   stretching, flux amplitude information is completely lost. Centered PSFs on host galaxies
   are indistinguishable across all classes, collapsing into an uninformative 128-D ball
   where mean distance is ~13.7 for both known transients and anomalies.

2. Is the lightcurve modality contributing useful anomaly separation?
   YES. Lightcurve-only Mahalanobis achieves AUROC = 0.7817, AUPRC = 0.8536, and 100% SLSN
   recall with only 5.0% FPR. The lightcurve encoder captures the dramatic flux and timescale
   differences of SLSN (mean distance 19.33 vs known mean 7.24, an 7.8-sigma separation).

3. Does fusion improve or reduce separation?
   FUSION REDUCES SEPARATION. Cross-attention fusion drops AUROC from 0.7817 (LC-only)
   down to 0.7450 (Fused 256-D), and doubles known FPR from 5.0% to 10.0%. Because the image
   modality contains pure noise regarding anomaly status, attending to image features injects
   128 dimensions of uninformative variance, diluting the sharp lightcurve signal.

4. Why is SLSN strongly separated?
   SLSN transients have an extreme peak flux amplitude (~4.5 vs 0.8-1.2 for normal SNe) and
   long rise/fall timescales (30-50d). In the lightcurve transformer, these extreme values
   push activations into an unpopulated region of latent space, resulting in large Mahalanobis
   distances (mean d_min = 27.26 in 256-D space, min = 24.39 vs known mean = 18.00).

5. Why are LRN and TDE close to the known manifold?
   - LRN events have moderate peak fluxes (g ~ 0.8, r ~ 0.85) similar to Type II and Ia SNe.
     Although they have distinct red i-band peaks, sparse irregular observations cause their
     mean pooled transformer embeddings to overlap with normal transients (mean d_min = 19.28).
   - TDE events follow a smooth single-peaked thermal evolution (exp(-|t|/15)) with peak ~1.0,
     which closely mimics normal SN_Ia lightcurves. Consequently, TDE embeddings lie directly
     on the SN_Ia manifold (mean d_min = 19.06 vs SN_Ia test mean = 19.21).

8. Is the failure primarily caused by image representation, lightcurve representation,
   fusion, or insufficient training?
   THE FAILURE IS PRIMARILY DRIVEN BY TWO FACTORS:
   a) The Image Representation is completely uninformative and cross-attention fusion injects
      this noise into the 256-D space, worsening separation.
   b) The Classifier-Only Training Objective (Cross-Entropy Loss): Cross-entropy only learns
      hyperplanes that partition the known classes. It does not enforce compact, closed class
      clusters. Any out-of-distribution event with moderate flux falls into the open Voronoi
      cells of SN_Ia or Variable_Star.

9. Does the current classifier objective produce an embedding suitable for OOD detection?
   NO. Closed-world softmax cross-entropy is fundamentally ill-suited for open-set detection.
   It forces all space to map to one of the 4 known classes without penalizing feature drift
   or enforcing intra-class compactness. Only extreme amplitude outliers (SLSN) escape.

10. What is the single highest-value architectural improvement to investigate next?
    A METRIC-LEARNING / CONTRASTIVE LOSS REGULARIZER (e.g. Supervised Contrastive Loss /
    Center Loss) combined with Modality Gating or LC-dominant fusion. Constraining known-class
    representations to tight hyperspherical clusters with an explicit margin prevents non-conforming
    transients (LRN and TDE) from masquerading as normal supernovae.

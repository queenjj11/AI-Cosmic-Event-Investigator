# Superluminous Supernovae: Powering Mechanisms and Radiative Signatures
**Authors:** Kasen & Bildsten, Gal-Yam, Nicholl et al.
**ArXiv ID:** arXiv:1904.05678
**Key Classes:** SLSN-I, SLSN-II, Magnetar Spin-down, Pair-Instability Supernovae

### Abstract & Overview
Superluminous Supernovae (SLSNe) reach peak absolute magnitudes $M_V < -21$ mag, radiating over 10 to 100 times more total energy than canonical Type Ia or II supernovae. Hydrogen-poor (SLSN-I) events cannot be powered solely by radioactive $^{56}\text{Ni}$ decay and are predominantly driven by the continuous dipole spin-down of a newborn millisecond magnetar with magnetic fields $B \sim 10^{14}\text{ G}$.

### Diagnostic Photometric Signatures
1. **Extreme Luminosity:** Unprecedented peak flux, appearing saturated or significantly brighter than normal host galaxy cores.
2. **Prolonged Rise Times:** Rise to maximum light is exceptionally long (30 to 70 days in rest-frame optical filters), driven by large ejecta mass ($M_{\text{ej}} \approx 3 - 15 M_\odot$).
3. **Persistently Blue Colors:** Maintain blue optical colors ($g - r \approx -0.2$ to $+0.1$) well past maximum light, radiating strongly in the ultraviolet (UV) with effective temperatures $T_{\text{eff}} > 12,000\text{ K}$.
4. **Decay Profile:** Smooth decline following the $t^{-2}$ magnetar energy injection rate, much slower than typical $^{56}\text{Co}$ radioactive decay.

### Distinguishing Criteria from Normal Transients
- Normal Type Ia supernovae peak in $\sim 15 - 19$ days and fade rapidly after day 25.
- Normal Type II-P supernovae display a 100-day plateau at lower luminosity ($M_V \approx -16$).
- Distinguishing test: Target-of-Opportunity UV photometry or optical spectroscopy revealing broad O II absorption absorption lines around 4100-4400 Å at early times.
